# Research-gap and research-question revision

## Proposed final research gaps

1. **Spatial support.** Multi-sensor comparisons can conflate sensor response with native spatial-support mismatch. A common FCOVER support must be controlled before agreement is interpreted.
2. **Spatial/environmental generalization.** A large sample in one AOI does not itself demonstrate transfer across contrasting plateau environments. The same pipeline must be replicated in selected, environmentally contrasting study areas.
3. **Temporal transferability.** Longer calibration histories add samples but may also introduce interannual heterogeneity and non-stationarity. Strict chronological evaluation is required to test, rather than assume, a monotonic benefit.

## Proposed final RQs (post-results manuscript)

- **RQ1.** Under a common 300 m FCOVER support, how consistently do sensor-specific NDVI-based FVC retrievals reproduce the Copernicus FCOVER product reference across selected environmentally contrasting study areas in Qinghai?
- **RQ2.** Does increasing historical calibration length from one to two or three years consistently improve forward-year FCOVER-reference agreement, or are effects sensor-, target-year-, and environment-dependent?
- **RQ3.** To what extent do development-stage spatial and interannual diagnostics agree with subsequent forward-year performance across sensors and study areas?

The current manuscript retains its evidence-matched endpoint comparison. After the extension, endpoint scaling should be positioned as a secondary operational baseline/sensitivity analysis under a different information condition: OLS uses historical FCOVER labels, while endpoint construction uses target-year NDVI distribution but no target-year FCOVER label. It is not a matched-supervision algorithm contest and is not proposed as the central novelty.
